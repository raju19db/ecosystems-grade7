➕ Operations with Integers

Addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, and order of operations with positive and negative whole numbers.

BC Math 7 · Number

🎬 Watch & Learn

Adding & Subtracting Integers

Khan Academy – clear visual explanation with number lines

Multiplying & Dividing Integers

Khan Academy – sign rules made simple

Order of Operations (BEDMAS)

Math Antics – BEDMAS with integers

✏️ Step-by-Step Examples

📘 Example 1 – Adding Integers with a Number Line

Problem: Calculate (−5) + (+8)

1

Start at −5 on the number line.

2

Adding a positive number means moving right by 8.

3

−5 + 8 = move right 8 from −5

−5 + 8 = +3
✅ Answer: +3
💡 Rule: When signs differ, subtract the smaller from the larger and keep the sign of the bigger number.
📘 Example 2 – Subtracting Integers

Problem: Calculate (−3) − (−7)

1

Subtracting a negative = adding a positive. Change the sign!

2

Rewrite: (−3) + (+7)

3

Different signs → subtract: 7 − 3 = 4. Keep the sign of the bigger (positive).

(−3) − (−7) = (−3) + (+7) = +4
✅ Answer: +4
💡 "Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive." Two negatives make a positive!
📘 Example 3 – Multiplying & Dividing Integers

Sign Rules: (+)(+) = +  |  (−)(−) = +  |  (+)(−) = −  |  (−)(+) = −

(−4) × (+5) = −20
(−6) × (−3) = +18
(−20) ÷ (+4) = −5
(−18) ÷ (−6) = +3
✅ Same signs → positive result. Different signs → negative result.
📘 Example 4 – BEDMAS with Integers

Problem: Evaluate: 3 + (−2) × 4 − (−1)

1

Brackets first: none here

2

Multiply: (−2) × 4 = −8

3

Rewrite: 3 + (−8) − (−1) = 3 − 8 + 1

4

Add/Subtract left to right: 3 − 8 = −5, then −5 + 1 = −4

3 + (−2) × 4 − (−1) = −4
✅ Answer: −4

✏️ Step-by-Step Examples

📘 Example 1 – Adding Decimals

Problem: 12.45 + 7.8

1

Line up the decimal points vertically.

2

Add zeros as placeholders: 12.45 + 7.80

12.45 + 7.80 ------ 20.25
✅ Answer: 20.25
📘 Example 2 – Multiplying Decimals

Problem: 2.4 × 1.5

1

Ignore decimals: multiply 24 × 15 = 360

2

Count decimal places: 2.4 has 1, 1.5 has 1 → total = 2 decimal places

3

Place decimal: 360 → 3.60

2.4 × 1.5 = 3.60
✅ Answer: 3.6
📘 Example 3 – Dividing Decimals

Problem: 8.4 ÷ 0.4

1

Multiply both by 10 to remove the decimal from the divisor: 84 ÷ 4

2

84 ÷ 4 = 21

8.4 ÷ 0.4 = 84 ÷ 4 = 21
✅ Answer: 21
📘 Example 4 – Order of Operations (BEDMAS)

Problem: 3.5 + 2.1 × 4 − 0.5

1

Multiply first: 2.1 × 4 = 8.4

2

Now: 3.5 + 8.4 − 0.5

3

Left to right: 3.5 + 8.4 = 11.9, then 11.9 − 0.5 = 11.4

3.5 + 2.1 × 4 − 0.5 = 11.4
✅ Answer: 11.4

✏️ Step-by-Step Examples

📘 Example 1 – Fraction to Decimal to Percent

Convert 3/4 to a decimal and a percent

1

Divide: 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75

2

Multiply by 100 for percent: 0.75 × 100 = 75%

3/4 = 0.75 = 75%
✅ Three ways to write the same value!
📘 Example 2 – Finding a Percent of a Number

Problem: What is 35% of 80?

1

Convert percent to decimal: 35% = 0.35

2

Multiply: 0.35 × 80 = 28

35% of 80 = 0.35 × 80 = 28
✅ Answer: 28
📘 Example 3 – Ratios

Problem: A class has 12 girls and 8 boys. Write the ratio of girls to boys in simplest form.

1

Write ratio: 12:8

2

Find GCF of 12 and 8: GCF = 4

3

Divide both: 12÷4 : 8÷4 = 3:2

12:8 = 3:2
✅ Answer: 3:2 (for every 3 girls there are 2 boys)

📈 Discrete Linear Relations

Using expressions, tables of values, and graphs to explore and describe linear patterns.

BC Math 7 · Algebra

🎬 Watch & Learn

Introduction to Linear Relations

Understanding patterns and tables of values

Graphing Linear Relations

Plotting points and identifying linear patterns

✏️ Step-by-Step Examples

📘 Example 1 – Table of Values

Pattern rule: y = 2x + 1. Complete the table.

x = 1 → y = 2(1)+1 = 3 x = 2 → y = 2(2)+1 = 5 x = 3 → y = 2(3)+1 = 7 x = 4 → y = 2(4)+1 = 9
✅ Each y goes up by 2 as x goes up by 1 — that's a LINEAR pattern!
📘 Example 2 – Writing the Expression

Problem: Tickets cost $3 each. Write an expression for the total cost for n tickets.

1

Cost per ticket × number of tickets

Cost = 3n
2

If there's a $2 service fee: Cost = 3n + 2

✅ This is a discrete linear relation — the cost goes up by $3 for every extra ticket.

⚖️ Two-Step Equations

Solving equations with whole-number coefficients, constants, and solutions.

BC Math 7 · Algebra

🎬 Watch & Learn

Two-Step Equations

Khan Academy – solving step by step

Algebra Basics – Solving Equations

Math Antics – balance method

✏️ Step-by-Step Examples

📘 Example 1 – Solve 2x + 5 = 13
1

Subtract 5 from both sides (undo the addition first)

2x + 5 − 5 = 13 − 5 → 2x = 8
2

Divide both sides by 2 (undo the multiplication)

2x ÷ 2 = 8 ÷ 2 → x = 4
3

Check: 2(4) + 5 = 8 + 5 = 13 ✓

✅ x = 4
📘 Example 2 – Solve 3x − 7 = 14
1

Add 7 to both sides: 3x = 21

2

Divide by 3: x = 7

3(7) − 7 = 21 − 7 = 14 ✓
✅ x = 7
💡 Always do the opposite operation. Addition ↔ Subtraction, Multiplication ↔ Division.
📘 Example 3 – Word Problem

Problem: Rudraansh has some books. After buying 3 more and doubling the total, he has 18 books. How many did he start with?

1

Let x = starting books. Equation: 2(x + 3) = 18

2

Divide both sides by 2: x + 3 = 9

3

Subtract 3: x = 6

✅ He started with 6 books.

⭕ Circumference & Area of Circles

Using π (pi) to calculate circumference and area — the constant ratio between circumference and diameter.

BC Math 7 · Geometry

🎬 Watch & Learn

Circumference of a Circle

Math Antics – understanding π and C = πd

Area of a Circle & Percents

Math Antics – area formula and equivalent fractions

✏️ Step-by-Step Examples

📘 Key Formulas
π ≈ 3.14 (or use the π button on your calculator) Circumference = π × d (where d = diameter) Circumference = 2 × π × r (where r = radius) Area = π × r² (r² means r × r)
💡 Radius = half of diameter. Diameter = 2 × radius.
📘 Example 1 – Find Circumference (diameter given)

Problem: Find the circumference of a circle with diameter 10 cm.

1

Formula: C = π × d

2

Substitute: C = 3.14 × 10

C = 3.14 × 10 = 31.4 cm
✅ Circumference ≈ 31.4 cm
📘 Example 2 – Find Area (radius given)

Problem: Find the area of a circle with radius 7 m.

1

Formula: A = π × r²

2

r² = 7 × 7 = 49

3

A = 3.14 × 49

A = 3.14 × 49 = 153.86 m²
✅ Area ≈ 153.86 m²

📦 Volume of Prisms & Cylinders

Calculating volume of rectangular prisms and cylinders.

BC Math 7 · Geometry

🎬 Watch & Learn

Volume of Rectangular Prisms

Math Antics – length × width × height

Volume of Cylinders

Khan Academy – using πr²h

✏️ Step-by-Step Examples

📘 Key Formulas
Rectangular Prism: V = l × w × h Cylinder: V = π × r² × h
💡 Volume of ANY prism or cylinder = Area of Base × Height
📘 Example 1 – Rectangular Prism

Problem: Find the volume of a box 8 cm long, 5 cm wide, 3 cm tall.

V = 8 × 5 × 3 = 120 cm³
✅ Volume = 120 cm³
📘 Example 2 – Cylinder

Problem: A can has radius 4 cm and height 12 cm. Find its volume.

1

r² = 4 × 4 = 16

2

V = 3.14 × 16 × 12

V = 3.14 × 16 × 12 = 602.88 cm³
✅ Volume ≈ 602.88 cm³

✏️ Step-by-Step Examples

📘 Example 1 – Plotting Points

A point (x, y) — x is horizontal, y is vertical. Start at origin (0,0).

Point A (3, −2): go RIGHT 3, then DOWN 2 → Quadrant IV Point B (−4, 1): go LEFT 4, then UP 1 → Quadrant II
💡 Quadrants: Q1 (+,+) Q2 (−,+) Q3 (−,−) Q4 (+,−)
📘 Example 2 – Translation

Translate point A (2,3) by (right 4, down 2):

New x = 2 + 4 = 6 New y = 3 − 2 = 1 A' = (6, 1)
✅ A moves to A' (6, 1)
📘 Example 3 – Reflection over the x-axis

Reflecting over the x-axis flips the y coordinate's sign.

P (3, 5) → P' (3, −5) Q (−2, 4) → Q' (−2, −4)
💡 Reflect over y-axis: flip the x sign. Reflect over x-axis: flip the y sign.

🥧 Circle Graphs

Reading, creating, and interpreting circle graphs (pie charts) to illustrate proportion.

BC Math 7 · Data

🎬 Watch & Learn

Reading Circle Graphs

Khan Academy – interpreting pie charts

Percents & Fractions for Circle Graphs

Math Antics – converting percents to fractions for pie charts

✏️ Step-by-Step Examples

📘 Example 1 – Percent to Degrees

Problem: 25% of students chose soccer. What angle does this sector use?

Angle = percent × 360° = 0.25 × 360° = 90°
✅ The soccer sector is 90°
💡 A full circle = 360°. So 50% = 180°, 25% = 90°, 10% = 36°.
📘 Example 2 – Reading a Circle Graph

A circle graph shows: Hockey 40%, Soccer 30%, Basketball 20%, Other 10%. Total students = 200.

Hockey: 40% × 200 = 80 students Soccer: 30% × 200 = 60 students Basketball: 20% × 200 = 40 students Other: 10% × 200 = 20 students
✅ Always multiply percent (as decimal) by the total to find the actual count.

🎲 Experimental Probability

Probability of two independent events — experimental vs theoretical probability.

BC Math 7 · Data & Probability

🎬 Watch & Learn

Basic Probability

Khan Academy – theoretical probability

Independent Events

Compound probability with two events

✏️ Step-by-Step Examples

📘 Key Concepts
Probability = (favourable outcomes) ÷ (total possible outcomes) P(event) ranges from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain)
💡 Two events are INDEPENDENT if one doesn't affect the other (e.g., flipping a coin twice).
📘 Example 1 – Single Event

Problem: Rolling a 6-sided die. What is P(rolling a 4)?

P(4) = 1/6 ≈ 0.167 ≈ 16.7%
✅ There is 1 favourable outcome out of 6 total outcomes.
📘 Example 2 – Two Independent Events

Problem: Flip a coin AND roll a die. What is P(heads AND rolling a 3)?

1

P(heads) = 1/2

2

P(rolling 3) = 1/6

3

Multiply (independent events): 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12

P(heads AND 3) = 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12 ≈ 8.3%
✅ Answer: 1/12

💰 Financial Literacy

Applying percents in financial contexts: tax, tips, discounts, and budgeting.

BC Math 7 · Financial Literacy

🎬 Watch & Learn

Percents – Tax, Tips & Discounts

Khan Academy – real-world percent applications

What Percent Is It? – Discounts & Tips

Math Antics – finding what percent one number is of another

✏️ Step-by-Step Examples

📘 Example 1 – Calculating Tax (BC GST + PST)

Problem: A video game costs $60. BC has 5% GST + 7% PST = 12% tax. What is the final price?

1

Tax amount = 12% × $60 = 0.12 × 60 = $7.20

2

Final price = $60 + $7.20

Final price = $67.20
✅ You pay $67.20
📘 Example 2 – Discount

Problem: Shoes originally cost $80 and are 30% off. What is the sale price?

1

Discount amount = 30% × $80 = $24

2

Sale price = $80 − $24 = $56

Sale price = $56
✅ You save $24, paying only $56.
📘 Example 3 – Tip at a Restaurant

Problem: Restaurant bill is $45. You want to leave a 15% tip. How much is the tip?

Tip = 15% × $45 = 0.15 × 45 = $6.75 Total = $45 + $6.75 = $51.75
✅ Tip = $6.75. Total bill = $51.75
